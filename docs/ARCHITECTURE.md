# SPECTRA-CQ: Model Architectures

## Overview
This repository implements a strict ablation study to evaluate the efficacy of Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) in Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) anomaly detection. The system compares a purely Classical Convolutional Autoencoder (CAE) against a Hybrid Quantum Autoencoder (HQAE). 

Both architectures share identical spatial encoders, decoders, and training protocols. The only independent variable is the mechanics of the latent bottleneck.

---

## 1. Classical Convolutional Autoencoder (CAE)

The classical baseline establishes the benchmark for Mean Squared Error (MSE) reconstruction on the 2D energy density scalograms generated by the Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT).

### Architecture Flow

**1. Input Space & Siamese Folding**
* Telemetry from uniaxial z-axis accelerometers is converted into a continuous energy matrix.
* In Phase 3 (Single Sensor) $\rightarrow$ the input tensor shape is `(Batch, 1, 64, 1000)`
* In Phase 4 (Multi-Sensor Spatial Fusion) $\rightarrow$ the input tensor shape is `(Batch, 6, 64, 1000)`. 
* **Siamese Update (For Phase 4):** The network temporarily folds the batch and channel dimensions together, transforming the shape to `(Batch * 6, 1, 64, 1000)`. All 6 sensors pass through the exact same convolutional filters (weight-tied parallel encoding). This extracts sensor-independent physical features without expanding the parameter count.

**2. Convolutional Encoder**
* The folded tensor passes through two `Conv2d` blocks:
   * **Conv1:** `(B*6, 16, 64, 1000)` via 3x3 kernels (stride 1, padding 1) $\rightarrow$ followed by BatchNorm $\rightarrow$ ReLU $\rightarrow$ and then `MaxPool2d` (kernel 2x4, stride 2x4) $\implies$ reducing dimensions to `(B*6, 16, 32, 250)`.
   * **Conv2:** `(B*6, 32, 32, 250)` via 3x3 kernels (stride 1, padding 1) $\rightarrow$ followed by BatchNorm $\rightarrow$ ReLU $\rightarrow$ and then `MaxPool2d` (kernel 4x10, stride 4x10) $\implies$ reducing dimensions to `(B*6, 32, 8, 25)`.
* The tensor is dynamically flattened to `(32 * 8 * 25)` $\implies$ yields a **6400-dimensional feature vector** per physical sensor.

**3. Classical Bottleneck (Latent Space)**
* A fully connected `Linear` layer aggressively compresses the 6400 features of *each* sensor down to just 2 continuous dimensions (`latent_dim_per_sensor`). 
* The output shape at the bottleneck is `(B*6, 2)`.

**4. Convolutional Decoder**
* A `Linear` layer projects the 2-dimensional vector back up to 6400 dimensions.
* The vector is reshaped back to the spatial dimensions `(B*6, 32, 8, 25)`:
   * **Transpose1:** `ConvTranspose2d` (kernel 4x10, stride 4x10) upsamples to `(B*6, 16, 32, 250)`, followed by BatchNorm and ReLU.
   * **Transpose2:** `ConvTranspose2d` (kernel 2x4, stride 2x4) upsamples to the original target shape of `(B*6, 1, 64, 1000)`.
* A final `Sigmoid` activation restricts output elements to `[0, 1]`.
* The tensor is unfolded back to the global multi-channel shape `(Batch, 6, 64, 1000)` to calculate reconstruction loss.

## 2. Hybrid Quantum Autoencoder (HQAE)

The HQAE replaces the classical dense bottleneck with a parameterized quantum layer.

By mapping the compressed features into an exponentially large Hilbert space, the model attempts to capture highly complex, non-linear structural degradation signatures using a fraction of trainable parameters.

### Architecture Flow (The Quantum Bottleneck)

The HQAE utilizes the exact same Siamese Convolutional Encoder to generate the 6400-dimensional feature vector per sensor. This vector is then passed into the VQC pipeline.

**1. Data Scaling + State Preparation (Embedding)**
* Classical data cannot natively exist inside a quantum processor. 
* A dense layer reduces the 6400-dimensional vector to the target qubit count (e.g., 2 dimensions per sensor $\implies$ 12 dimensions total across the 6-sensor graph).
* **Crucial Scaling:** The classical vector is strictly bounded to the range `[-π, π]` to prevent periodicity collisions (phase wrapping) on the Bloch sphere.
* **Angle Embedding:** The PennyLane circuit uses `qml.RX`. Each scaled classical float dictates the exact X-axis rotational phase of a corresponding qubit. The classical values are mapped 1:1 onto the qubits.

**2. The Ansatz (Parameterized Quantum Circuit)**
The core trainable mechanism consists of specific unitary operations:
* **Rotational Gates:** 
   * The circuit applies trainable `qml.RX`, `qml.RY` and `qml.RZ` gates to the qubits. 
   * The classical Adam optimizer updates the specific rotation angles (`weights`) via backpropagation.
* **Entanglement Topology:** 
   * The qubits are entangled based on the specific structural graph ablation.
   * This links the probability amplitudes $\leftarrow$ theoretically allows the network to capture spatial correlations between physical sensor nodes.

**3. Measurement (Expectation Values)**
* The quantum state must collapse to return data to the classical decoder.
* The PennyLane node measures the expectation value of the Pauli-Z operator (`qml.expval(qml.PauliZ(wire))`) for the qubits.
* This yields a deterministic classical vector bounded between `[-1, 1]`.
* This vector is passed to the exact same Classical Decoder described in Section 1 (Classical CAE).

## 3. The Entanglement Ablation Matrix

To rigorously isolate the cause of gradient decay and performance limits, the HQAE Ansatz is subjected to a multi-axis topology ablation study. 

### Phase 2 and 3 Topologies
1. **`none`:** Zero entanglement. Employs independent rotations without multi-qubit gates.
2. **`basic`:** Localized ring entanglement using `qml.CNOT`.
3. **`strong`:** Global all-to-all entanglement using `qml.CNOT`.

### Phase 4 Spatial Topologies (12-Qubit Physical Graph)
In Phase 4, the 12 qubits are physically mapped to the 6 telemetry nodes: PE11 (0,1), PE12 (2,3), PE13 (4,5), PE21 (6,7), PE22 (8,9), and PE23 (10,11). The topologies map connections strictly between adjacent physical nodes and cross-span girders.

1. **`bridge_graph_hard`:**
   * Entangles the physical graph utilizing unparameterized `qml.CNOT` gates.
   * A CNOT gate executes a rigid binary flip on the target qubit based on the control qubit. 
   * Applying CNOTs rigidly across distant physical nodes mathematically severed continuous gradient flow during backpropagation, leading to Barren Plateaus.

2. **`bridge_graph_soft`:**
   * Entangles the exact same physical graph, but replaces CNOT gates with parameterized `qml.CRY` (Controlled-Y Rotation) gates.
   * A `CRY` gate applies a continuous rotation to the target qubit based on a trainable weight (`weights[layer, current_weight_idx]`).
   * This parameterization is the architectural mechanism that enables the Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) penalty.
   * It allows the optimizer to continuously tune the entanglement strength between distant sensors $\implies$ accurately mapping macroscopic wave propagation without gradient vanishing.

## 4. Evaluation Protocol: Synthetic Anomaly Injection

Because physical load tests representing high-frequency degradation are unavailable in the baseline 2024 OpenLAB dataset, the architectures are evaluated using synthetic anomaly injection. We evaluate the models against five progressive noise limits representing varying stages of structural fatigue:

* **2% Noise (0.02):**
   * Extreme early-stage micro-cracking
   * (Incipient damage hidden entirely within the environmental noise floor)
* **3% Noise (0.03):** 
   * Developing micro-cracks
   * (The physical inflection point where the damage signature begins to mathematically separate from ambient variance)
* **4% Noise (0.04):**
   * Pre-yield stiffness loss
   * (Coalescing micro-cracks immediately prior to the emergence of macroscopic deterioration)
* **5% Noise (0.05):**
   * Intermediate localized degradation
   * (Active structural deterioration, e.g., initial concrete spalling or minor localized yielding)
* **10% Noise (0.10):**
   * Advanced localized degradation
   * (Severe localized failure nearing a catastrophic loss of load-bearing capacity)

Both models are frozen and evaluated on an unseen test set to generate comparative AUC-ROC sensitivities.

## 5. Training Protocol and Constraints

To ensure rigorous academic reproducibility and account for the mathematical realities of Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) algorithms, both models strictly adhere to the following protocol:

* **Objective Function:**
   * Mean Squared Error (MSE) reconstruction loss is used during training to optimize the autoencoder weights.
   * Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC) is calculated to measure anomaly detection sensitivity during evaluation.
   * In Phase 4, the `bridge_soft` architecture dynamically modifies the loss function by adding a continuous Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) penalty. This mathematically enforces the physical spatial continuity across the 6-sensor graph.
* **Optimization Landscape:**
   * Quantum neural networks are highly susceptible to Barren Plateaus.
   * To account for this rugged optimization topology, all models are strictly bounded to a 15-Epoch training phase.
* **Deterministic Evaluation:**
   * To mitigate initialization bias, the evaluation protocol averages the MSE convergence and AUC-ROC scores across an ensemble of 3 distinct deterministic seeds.
